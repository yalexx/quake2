# RTX-style raster restyle — reconciled build

This is a WebGL2/GLES3 raster treatment, **not ray tracing**. It extends the
shaders the shipped Qwasm2 build already compiles (`rtx-renderer.js`), using the
reviewed material identities in `rtx-materials.js` (generated by
`scripts/rtx-materials.mjs`). No engine binary, PAK, texture or art file is
changed, and the game still boots straight into `demo1`.

The previous raster pass (`cb01021`, from `clawbox/run-ki10637r` / `run-fc045b0s`)
is the baseline this build reconciles. Its world hook, material catalog, scene
copy and Off/Performance/High control are retained; its weaker lighting and
composite are corrected, and two surfaces it never reached — the engine's flat
sky fill and textured models — are now covered.

## What the renderer now does

**World surfaces** (`rtx-renderer.js`, `q2Surface`). The engine supplies the
sampled albedo and the baked lightmap (which already contains the dynamic
lights). The pass redistributes that existing energy; it adds no light, shadow
or geometry.

- A two-lobe specular response (a tight dielectric glint over a broad, rougher
  sheen) whose roughness comes from the reviewed material class and the
  texture's own luminance. Metal, tech, rust, stone and crate surfaces separate.
- Inferred relief from the albedo's screen-space gradient, scaled per material
  (stone and rust keep more, lamps and glass almost none). This is relief read
  from the picture, not an authored normal map or displacement.
- A cool-overhead / warm-floor hemisphere term on the existing baked energy, so
  up-facing and down-facing surface read differently.
- Restrained aerial perspective beyond 620 world units, tinted by the surface's
  own baked light; High carries about three times Performance's haze.
- Fixture emission from bright pixels of lamp-classified textures, stronger in
  High, carried by the composite bloom. It does not cast light.
- Dynamic-light specular in High, from the engine's real light positions.
- Highlight compression before the LDR framebuffer clips.

**Models and the view weapon** (`q2ModelShade`). The engine lights models by one
vertex colour, so they read flat. The model vertex shader now passes a world
normal, world position and eye position to the model fragment shader, which adds
a small key-light shape, a cool fresnel rim and a bounded metal sheen. Weapon
and monster skins are not in the wall-texture catalog, so they share one
restrained model profile instead of a guessed material.

**Sky.** In this build the demo's sky is drawn by the engine as a flat
two-dimensional fill; its skybox files do not load. The pass therefore tints
that existing fill with a screen-vertical gradient derived from the fill's own
colour: deeper and slightly cooler overhead, warmer at the horizon. No sky
texture, cubemap or art is added. When Off, the fill is passed through
untouched.

**Composite.** The finished scene is copied once per frame before the HUD and
graded: cool-shadow / warm-light split, a small lifted toe, saturation, a
two-tap bloom in Performance and a 12+6 tap halo in High, a very light corner
falloff in High, and a one-level interleaved dither that reduces lightmap and sky
banding. The HUD, menus, console text and crosshair are drawn after this pass
and stay crisp and unbloomed.

## Reconciliation of the earlier work

| Earlier technique | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook the GLES3 world shader pair, add `q2ViewDirection` in the vertex shader, scene copy at the first 2D draw, restore every GL binding and state | **Retained unchanged** | It is the only integration point this build exposes; Off restoration is pixel-exact. |
| Material catalog keyed by upload size + 64 sampled pixels, generated from both PAKs | **Retained, generator committed** | `scripts/rtx-materials.mjs --check` reproduces `rtx-materials.js` (594 identities) and touches no PAK. |
| Single-lobe specular (`pow(ndh, mix(96,7))`) | **Corrected** | Rough metal read as one flat wash. Replaced with a tight+broad two-lobe mix. |
| Relief perturbation clamped to ±0.14 at a fixed strength | **Corrected** | One strength for stone, rust and polished metal flattened the hard surfaces and over-shaded the smooth ones. Now per material. |
| No ambient/hemisphere term | **Added** | The lightmap is a single scalar-ish energy; the cool/warm hemisphere split gives the shape a direction without inventing light. |
| Emissive lamps `emitterColor = albedo*2.6`, one bloom radius | **Corrected** | High now pushes the fixture core harder and uses a wide halo plus a tight core; Performance keeps the two-tap fallback measured to hold the frame budget. |
| `mix(vec3(y), c, 1.10)` grade, no dither | **Corrected** | The grade banded on 8-bit dark corridors and across the sky. Dither 1 level and a light High vignette; saturation 1.12. |
| Nothing done for models or the flat sky fill | **Added** | They cover much of the first-person frame. Models now get volume and a metal sheen; the sky fill gets a gradient. |
| Aerial haze 0.10 / 0.30 with a fixed 620–2500 range | **Retained** | Restrained by design; near geometry is untouched. |
| Dynamic-light specular in High | **Retained** | Uses the engine's real light positions, bounded by existing energy. |
| Page-side HUD fade from the run-ki10637r commit (`hud.js`) | **Omitted** | It is an unrelated HUD change sharing that commit; this task preserves the existing HUD and does not alter 2D alpha. |
| Ray tracing, path tracing, GI, new shadow maps, authored PBR channels, engine rebuild | **Still unavailable** | The shipped `ref_gles3.wasm` exposes no such path; no engine source or toolchain is in the checkout. Nothing here claims them. |

## Toggle honesty

`Lighting -> RTX-style` sets one uniform (`q2RTX`) in every patched program.
When it is Off, every added branch is skipped and the composite is not drawn. The
measurement below toggled Off → Performance → High → Off at the same paused
camera and compared the first and last frames: **mean absolute RGB difference 0,
0 changed pixels** on 1,049,088 pixels. Off is the original frame, not an
approximation of it.

## Measured cost

Measured in the device's visible kiosk Chromium (hardware GLES3,
`ANGLE (Intel, Mesa Intel(R) UHD Graphics 620 (KBL GT2), OpenGL ES 3.2)`,
WebGL2, drawing buffer 1366x768) with `node scripts/texture-fps.mjs --rtx`.
The camera is paused (the harness binds F10 to `pause`), so every mode draws the
same scene. Frame work is timed inside the engine's own `MainLoop_runner` with
`gl.finish`; capped fps is `gl.clear` cadence. One-minute load 3.0–3.5 on 8
cores during the samples.

| Mode | Capped fps | Frame interval median / p95 (ms) | Work per frame median / p95 (ms) |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Off | 74.84 | 13.3 / 13.8 | 1.6 / 2.3 |
| Performance | 74.99 | 13.4 / 13.8 | 4.9 / 6.7 |
| High | 74.69 | 13.3 / 13.9 | 4.2 / 8.2 |

The display cap (about 13.35 ms per frame at 75 Hz) is what the capped fps
measures; it cannot separate the modes. The work numbers can: Performance adds
about **3.3 ms/frame** over Off (median), High about **2.6 ms** (its median sits
inside the sample noise, its p95 is 5.9 ms over Off). Both leave roughly
**8.4 ms/frame** of the 13.35 ms display interval unused at this resolution and
GPU — inside the 13.141 ms budget measured for this build in
`docs/texture-review/REALISM.md`. No uncapped-fps claim is made.

Off-vs-Performance at the same paused camera changed **17.8%** of pixels by more
than 8 RGB levels (mean absolute difference 6.51); Off-vs-High changed 21.2%.
No new clipped white pixels appear (0.001% in both modes).

## Static-view detail checks

Same-eye Off/High pairs taken in the live game (1366x768, hardware), with the
console and menu closed. Laplacian energy is the mean 3x3 Laplacian magnitude of
luminance over the frame; a higher value means more local structure.

| View | Mean abs diff | Pixels changed >8 levels | Laplacian Off -> High | Pure-white share Off -> High |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| demo1 sky brush | 10.44 | 46.8% | 1.61 -> 2.72 | 0.001% -> 0.001% |
| demo1 riveted wall | 5.43 | 10.8% | 3.12 -> 4.07 | 0.06% -> 0.06% |
| demo1 corridor floor | 13.55 | 51.4% | 4.11 -> 5.18 | 0.06% -> 0.001% |

The sky's Off picture is a flat fill; in High the same pixels run from a cooler,
deeper value overhead to a warmer horizon (for example 104,64,25 at the top to
104,72,0 lower down, against a flat 115,69,0 in Off). No clipping was added.

## Reproduce

```
node scripts/rtx-materials.mjs --check
node scripts/texture-fps.mjs --rtx --cdp http://127.0.0.1:18801 \
  --live http://127.0.0.1:4288 --maps demo1 --out <evidence dir>/rtx
node scripts/tex-check.mjs
node scripts/texture-pack.mjs --check
```

The game is served from this checkout by `node server.js` at
`http://127.0.0.1:4288/` (also under `/apps/quake2/`); a visible hardware
Chromium exposes CDP on 18801. The `--rtx` mode navigates, checks the renderer
(WebGL2, ANGLE, `vid_renderer gles3`, 1366x768), captures Off / Performance /
High / Off-restored at one paused camera, and writes `fps.json` plus the PNGs.

## Limits

- No ray tracing, GI, shadow maps, reflections of scene geometry, authored
  normal/roughness/emission maps or engine rebuild. The broad key and hemisphere
  terms are art-directed approximations bounded by the existing lightmap.
- The sky gradient is screen-vertical because the engine's flat sky fill carries
  no view direction; it is a tint of the existing fill, not a skybox.
- Model skins are not classified by the wall-texture catalog; models share one
  restrained profile.
- The relief is inferred from the albedo and its screen-space derivatives, so it
  is sharp on flat faces and softer at grazing angles; it is not displacement.
- A very large flat sky can still show faint 8-bit steps: the one-level dither
  reduces the banding, it does not remove it.
- There is no anti-aliasing; geometry and model edges keep the engine's jaggies,
  and the HUD is the original low-resolution 2D art.
- Measurements are on one GPU at 1366x768; a larger buffer or a different GPU
  needs its own measurement. The box is shared and was not idle.
