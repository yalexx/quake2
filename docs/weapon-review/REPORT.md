## What was measured

- **In game.** The checkout's own `server.js` on a spare port, with a headless Chromium of this run on a spare debugging port (no dependency on the live server on 4231 or the kiosk on 18801/9222). `demo1` is loaded at 1280x720 with the app's normal arguments plus `+set r_mode 14` (1280x720). After `sv_cheats 1; give all`, each weapon is put in hand with `use <item>`, the console is closed, `keyDest` is checked back at game and the frame is captured with the page's own chrome (Options/Lighting chips, pointer-lock prompt, fullscreen button) hidden by a page-only style element. The engine's HUD/status bar is untouched and visible.
- **Counters.** Every capture asserts the RTX renderer's per-skin-size `modelTextureDraws` advanced for the size the engine bound, that `weaponDraws` and the neutral-shading counter (`weaponNeutralDraws`, the t4 path) advanced, and that the frame equals the weapon the crosshair code names from the bound skin size. `lastModelSkinDraws` is recorded too, but it is only incremented for the skin of the frame's last model draw, so it can legitimately stay flat when the game draws an item or monster after the gun; it is not part of the assertion. A blank or wrong gun frame would fail the skin-draw and the gun-pixel checks below.
- **Gun pixels.** A second capture with `cl_gun 0` in the same frame shows the scene without the gun; the changed pixels are the drawn weapon. This proves the shot is not blank and gives the in-game saturation sample.
- **Texture metrics.** The 13 pak10 skins and the 13 pak0 originals are decoded with the project's PCX reader (row length = width, matching the engine) and compared: mean HSV saturation and mean chroma over opaque texels, index-255 counts and the exact 2x2 cut-out mask, fringe colours on the cut-out boundary, and a 2-texel blockiness ratio (boundary luma step / inside-block luma step), whole skin and pistol-bottom band.
- **Project passes.** `tex-check`, `texture-pack --dry-run` and `texture-audit --select` are run from this checkout and their exit codes and summaries recorded.

## Assumptions

- The three extra view models (`v_flareg`, `v_disint`, `v_handgr`) have no item in `game_baseq2.wasm`; `use` answers `unknown item: …`. Their captures therefore use the test-only substituted pak10 described above. The frame's RTX name lists every model whose skin has that size, so it reads `v_blast/v_flareg`, `v_bfg/v_disint/v_shotg`, `v_handgr/v_machn`; the extra weapon's own name is present and its 2x skin size is drawn, and the screenshot is the extra weapon, distinguished from the slot's real weapon by the pair-difference check.
- `v_flareg`'s skin ships as `base.pcx`, which `rtx-renderer.js`'s size index deliberately does not scan (it indexes `skin.pcx` only). The test pak adds a `models/weapons/v_flareg/skin.pcx` entry with the same bytes so the renderer can name the size; the engine never loads that added entry.
- In-game saturation compares the rendered gun pixels of the 2x session with a baseline session whose pak10 weapon entries hold the pak0 originals (test-only archive, same delivery path). It is a rendered-pixel measurement: lighting (the neutral weapon path) and the palette are part of it, which is the point of an end-to-end check.
- Saturation is `(max−min)/max` per pixel, the same definition `model-art.mjs` uses; chroma is `max−min`. Means are per opaque texel, so the 2x skin and the 1x original weigh their own pixels equally.
- The blockiness ratio is measured at 2-texel period: an inside-block step (even x) versus a block-boundary step (odd x), averaged over the skin (and over the lower 40% for the pistol). A nearest-neighbour 2x upscale makes the inside step zero; a smoothed upscale keeps the two comparable.
- `r_mode 14` is the engine's 1280x720 mode (from its own mode strings); the page and canvas are asserted at 1280x720 before any capture.
